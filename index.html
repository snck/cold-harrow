Coldharrow

100 kilometres. 4,500 metres of climbing. One night, one day.
It never always gets worse.

Mouse and keyboard. Nothing is timed: take as long as you like over every decision.

How to play

The race

The Coldharrow Traverse: 100 km, starting at midnight. Run through the night over a 2,140 m ridge, through a hot afternoon, and home to Lowmere. Aid stations have cutoff times; miss one and your race is over.

Each section, you choose

  • Effort: Walk, Easy, Steady or Push. On climbs that means power-hiking vs running; on descents, cautious vs bombing it. Every option shows what it will cost.
  • Fuel plan: how much you eat from your vest each hour. Too little and you'll bonk hours from now. Too much and your stomach revolts.
  • Water: drink to thirst, or ration it.
  • Quick actions: jacket, salt, a gel, caffeine, and your reasons.

Then you watch the section unfold. Things happen on the trail: weather, falls, other runners, your body and your mind. Each one stops and asks you what to do.

Body and mind

Body Energy, hydration, stomach, legs, feet, core temperature, alertness. Fix these with food, water, effort and kit.

Mind Morale is how you feel right now. Resolve is your will to go on: it drains while you're in a dark place, and if it runs out you will stop. Company, views, sunrise, your reasons and fixing your body all help.

Hover over (or tap) any bar to see why it's moving.

Consequences arrive late

Bombing a descent feels great now. Your quads will tell you about it two hours later. Skipping food is free until it isn't. Plan ahead: the elevation profile is always there: hover over it, or touch and drag along it.

Aid stations

Refill, eat, ice, sit, sleep, see the medic, open your drop bag. Every action costs time. You can also hand in your bib. Sometimes that's the right call.
